Digital Full Mouth Panoramic X-Ray

A single panoramic image of your entire jaw, teeth, sinuses, and TMJ joints — essential for comprehensive diagnosis, implant planning, and orthodontic assessment.

Overview

Complete Jaw Imaging

A panoramic X-ray (also known as an OPG — Orthopantomogram) captures a single, wide-view image of your entire mouth in one shot. Unlike individual intraoral X-rays that show just a few teeth at a time, a panoramic X-ray displays all upper and lower teeth, the jawbones, sinuses, nasal area, and temporomandibular (TMJ) joints in a single comprehensive image.

At Dr. Kathuria's Dentistry, we use the latest digital panoramic X-ray technology that produces high-resolution images with minimal radiation exposure. The procedure is quick, comfortable, and non-invasive — the machine rotates around your head while you stand still, capturing the complete image in under 20 seconds.

  • Full view of both jaws in a single image
  • Essential for dental implant treatment planning
  • Quick and comfortable — under 20 seconds
  • Low radiation digital technology
  • No sensor placed inside the mouth
Applications

When Is a Panoramic X-Ray Needed?

Dental Implant Planning

Panoramic X-rays reveal jawbone height, width, and density, nerve canal locations, and sinus proximity — all critical information for safe and precise implant placement.

Wisdom Teeth Evaluation

OPG images clearly show impacted, partially erupted, or misaligned wisdom teeth and their relationship to nerves and adjacent teeth, guiding extraction decisions.

Orthodontic Assessment

Before braces or aligners, a panoramic X-ray helps evaluate tooth positions, unerupted teeth in children, root alignment, and jaw symmetry for treatment planning.

General Screening

Used as a comprehensive screening tool to detect cysts, tumours, infections, bone loss, and jaw disorders that may not be visible on regular intraoral X-rays.

Advantages

Benefits of Digital Panoramic X-Ray

  • Captures entire mouth in one quick, painless image
  • No uncomfortable sensor placed inside the mouth
  • Lower radiation dose than a full set of individual X-rays
  • Detects hidden problems not visible in regular X-rays
  • Digital format allows enhancement, storage, and sharing
  • Ideal for patients with gag reflex or limited mouth opening
